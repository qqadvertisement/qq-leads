# Outreach voice rules

Drafts are a **starting point Angela edits before sending** — never a finished message, never
sent by the machine. When she edits one, the diff plus her reason goes into
`data/feedback.json`, and that's how this file grows.

---

## Structure: five beats

1. **Route disclosed** — how you came to be looking at their page. A message that lists a
   defect without saying how you found it reads as surveillance.
2. **One customer-visible wall** — the single most *visible* defect, not the most damning
   one. Listing multiple defects reads as a compiled report rather than something a person
   happened to hit.
3. **Mechanical consequence** — blame the gap, never the owner. "When someone can't find your
   menu in a few seconds, they pick the spot next door." Never "you're probably losing
   customers" — that diagnoses their business, which you have no standing to do.
4. **Who you are** — one line. Rotate the phrasing across a batch so owners who compare notes
   don't see an identical paragraph.
5. **Permission-style ask** — "want to see?" Not a hard CTA.

*Provenance: fb-2026-08-21-004 (v1 drafts read as surveillance)*

---

## Hard constraints

- **No pricing.** Not in any draft, ever, at any beat.
- **No invented owner name.** No owner name has been confirmed for any lead across every run
  to date. Open with "Hey!" or a restaurant-specific detail. The model draft's "Hey Maria!"
  is an illustration of warmth, not a licence to guess a name.
- **One genuine specific detail** about their place — proof you actually looked.
- 3–5 sentences. Contractions. Plain words. Warm, low-pressure, a little casual.

## Banned phrasings (AI/corporate tells)

"I hope this finds you well" · "I wanted to reach out" · "leverage" · "solutions" · "elevate" ·
"in today's digital landscape" · "unlock" · "seamless" · fake enthusiasm · emoji spam ·
long dashes strung through sentences

## Reference draft

> Hey Maria! Love the al pastor at The Corner Taqueria — genuinely one of my favorites in the
> neighborhood. Quick thing: I went to pull up your menu online and couldn't find it anywhere,
> just your Instagram. When folks are deciding where to eat and can't find a menu in a few
> seconds, they usually just pick the next place, even when yours is better. I build simple
> sites for Chicago spots, plus a little game your regulars can play to win a free taco that
> quietly builds you a list of customers to bring back. Happy to mock up what yours could look
> like, no strings — want to see?

Match the warmth. Personalize beats 1–2 to the real, verified gap.
